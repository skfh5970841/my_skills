# Chaesajang-Style Principles

Use these as underlying principles, not imitation rules.

## Perception Rearrangement

The goal is the moment when the reader thinks, "So this was connected that way." Style comes from arranging concepts so the reader reaches that recognition.

## Argument by Placement

Do not merely announce a claim. Place elements so the structure itself implies the judgment.

## Structural Convergence

A personal scene, common belief, historical event, or concept should eventually be located inside a larger structure.

## Cross-Field Axis

History, economics, politics, philosophy, and science become powerful when they are tied by one axis, not listed as separate fields.

## Comfortable Confidence

Use a conversational but assured voice. Avoid both academic distance and apologetic uncertainty.

## Reader Position

Stand beside the reader. The writing should help the reader see, not command the reader to agree.

## Rhythm

Short sentences can create tension. Longer sentences can unfold concepts. Repetition is useful only until the rhythm has completed its work.

## Breaking Structure

The style is not a fixed order. It is the judgment to know when a question, scene, paradox, concept, or return is stronger than the expected next step.
